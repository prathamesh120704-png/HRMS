# Interview guide (RESEARCH.md §8): 15 minutes

The assignment asks you to interview **one working professional** about the HR system they use, and write what they dislike.
The answers must be theirs. This guide helps you ask good questions; it does not replace the conversation.

## 1. Who to ask
Anyone who works for a company with a leave and attendance system: a parent, an aunt or uncle, a senior from college, a neighbour.
Best: someone who applies for leave themselves, or a manager who approves it. A person in HR is a bonus but not required.
Avoid: someone who has never used a leave or attendance system.

## 2. Before you call (2 minutes)
- Ask permission: "It's for my college project. Can I ask you 10 questions about the leave and attendance system at your office? It takes about 15 minutes. I'll only use your first name, or 'a relative', unless you say otherwise."
- Do **not** ask for names of colleagues, salary, or any ID numbers. You do not need them.
- Have [RESEARCH.md](../RESEARCH.md) §8 open to fill in straight afterwards, while you remember.

## 3. The questions
Ask open questions ("tell me about…"), then stay quiet and let them talk. Write down their words.

**Warm-up**
1. What do you do, and roughly how many people work at your company?
2. What do you use to apply for leave and to mark attendance (an app, a website, Excel, WhatsApp, a register)?

**Leave**
3. Walk me through the last time you applied for leave. What did you do, step by step?
4. How long did it take to get an answer? How did you find out?
5. Have you ever been unsure how much leave you had left, or been shown a wrong number? What happened?
6. If you take Friday and Monday off, how many days are taken from your leave? (This tests whether they have a "sandwich rule", RESEARCH.md §9.)
7. What happens to leave you don't use by the end of the year?

**Attendance**
8. What happens if you are late, or forget to punch in or out? Who fixes it, and how long does it take?
9. Do holidays differ by office or city at your company? How do you know which apply to you?

**The big one**
10. **What do you dislike most about the system? What makes you groan?**
11. If you could change one thing first, what would it be?
12. Do you use it on your phone? Is that easy?

If they are a **manager**, add: How do you know who is away this week? Have you ever approved leave and later found out the person had no balance?

## 4. While they talk
- Quote them in their own words wherever you can ("It shows the balance only after HR updates it on the 5th").
- When they mention a problem, ask **"what did you do instead?"** and **"how often does that happen?"**
- Don't suggest answers. "Is it slow?" puts words in their mouth. "How did it feel?" doesn't.
- Don't explain your project's features while you are still asking.

## 5. After the call (10 minutes): write §8 in your own words
Fill in RESEARCH.md §8:
1. **Who, date, how** (anonymised).
2. **What they told me:** 3 to 5 plain sentences, with one or two short quotes.
3. **What they dislike most:** the single biggest complaint.
4. **What surprised me.**
5. **What I changed or decided because of it.** This is the part that earns the marks. Link each thing to your app. Examples of the kind of link (use only what is true for **your** interview):
   - They were unsure of their balance → why Tarang holds pending days and shows opening / accrued / used / pending / available (rule R21).
   - Approvals take days → the manager's approvals inbox on the home screen, plus emails (rule L6).
   - They forgot to punch → the correction request (regularisation) and the "missed punch" flag (rule R25).
   - They have no phone access → the bottom tab bar and the phone layouts.
6. If nothing they said changed anything, say so honestly and explain why. That is still a valid finding.

## 6. Be ready to be asked
- "Who did you interview and what did they tell you?"
- "What is the one thing in your app that came from that conversation?"
Know the answer without notes. Do not claim the interview influenced a feature unless it actually did.

## 7. The very short version (a WhatsApp or voice-note chat, about 5 minutes)
If a call is not possible, send this to **one real person** who works at a company with a leave and attendance system, and use their **actual replies**:

> Hi, I am doing a college project on HR systems and need 5 minutes of your help. I will only use your first name (or "a relative"). Could you answer these in a message or voice note?
> 1. What do you use at work to apply for leave and mark attendance?
> 2. Tell me about the last time you applied for leave: what did you do, and how long until you got an answer?
> 3. Have you ever been unsure how much leave you had left, or seen a wrong number?
> 4. What happens when you forget to punch in or out?
> 5. What do you dislike most about it?

Paste what they wrote (or the key lines from the voice note) into RESEARCH.md §8 as their words, with their permission, then fill in the table of what you changed because of it. Do not edit their words to say something they did not say.
